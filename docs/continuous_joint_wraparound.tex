\title{Continuous--Joint Wraparound in the Compliant Effort Controller\\
\large A mathematical analysis of the ``random spinning'' failure}
\author{compliant\_controllers --- Kinova Gen3 (7\,DoF)}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This note derives, from first principles, why a joint can occasionally
``run away'' (spin a full turn) when the arm is driven by the compliant
\emph{effort} controller. The mechanism is a mismatch between the angle
convention used for the \emph{measured} state and the convention used for
the \emph{desired} (commanded) state: the measured state is unwrapped into
a continuous frame with a per--joint integer offset, while the desired
state is consumed in the trajectory controller's own (raw) frame. The
resulting constant $2\pi$ position error is multiplied by the joint
stiffness and applied as an \emph{unclamped} torque, which drives the
joint around. We give the algebra, identify the exact source terms in the
code, separate what is verified from what is assumed, and state the fix.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and honesty notes}

The controller specialises \texttt{hardware\_interface::EffortJointInterface}
only; there is no position/velocity variant. Hence the analysis below applies
exactly when the arm is in \emph{effort control mode}. In any position mode the
control law of Section~\ref{sec:law} does not run and the robot's onboard
controller resolves wraparound, which is consistent with the observation that
the symptom appears only in effort mode.

Two facts are \textbf{verified directly from the source tree}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the measured state receives a one--shot $+2\pi$ shift and is then
        unwrapped into a continuous frame (Section~\ref{sec:ext});
  \item the desired state is consumed \emph{raw} and the proportional
        position error is \emph{unclamped} (Section~\ref{sec:law}).
\end{itemize}

One ingredient is \textbf{assumed and flagged for verification}
(Section~\ref{sec:jtc}): the precise angle convention of (a) the upstream
\texttt{joint\_trajectory\_controller} for \texttt{continuous} joints and
(b) the kortex hardware driver's reported range. These determine
\emph{how often} the failure offset is non--zero, not \emph{whether} the
mechanism exists.

\section{Notation}

For joint $i$ (the \texttt{continuous} joints on the Gen3 are
$i\in\{1,3,5,7\}$):
\begin{align*}
\phi^m_i(t)\in\R &\quad\text{true (unbounded) measured physical angle},\\
\phi^d_i(t)\in\R &\quad\text{true (unbounded) desired physical angle},\\
q^{hw}_i(t)      &\quad\text{hardware reading }\equiv\phi^m_i \!\!\pmod{2\pi},\ \text{wrapped},\\
\Delta t         &\quad\text{control period},\qquad \Theta=\tfrac{3\pi}{2}\ \text{wrap threshold}.
\end{align*}
The normalisation used by the code
(\texttt{src/extended\_joint\_positions.cpp:96}) is
\begin{equation}
\Nrm(x)=\big((x+\pi)\bmod 2\pi\big)-\pi\ \in[-\pi,\pi).
\end{equation}
The adapter shift (\texttt{hardware\_interface\_adapter\_impl.h:140}),
applied once per cycle to every joint, is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shift}
\tilde q_i = q^{hw}_i + 2\pi\,\big[\,q^{hw}_i<0\,\big],
\qquad \tilde q_i\equiv\phi^m_i\!\!\pmod{2\pi}.
\end{equation}

\section{The extended (measured) position}
\label{sec:ext}

With stored raw value $c_i$ (\texttt{current\_}) and accumulator
$\theta_i$ (\texttt{diff\_}), one \texttt{update(t)} performs
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:update}
\theta_i \leftarrow
\begin{cases}
\theta_i+\Nrm(t)-\Nrm(c_i), & |t-c_i|\ge\Theta \quad(\text{``wrap''}),\\[4pt]
2\pi\big\lfloor \theta_i/2\pi\big\rfloor + t, & |t-c_i|<\Theta \quad(\text{``no wrap''}),
\end{cases}
\qquad c_i\leftarrow t .
\end{equation}
The integer count in the source --- \texttt{static\_cast<int>($\theta/2\pi$)}
with a $-1$ correction for negatives --- equals $\lfloor\theta_i/2\pi\rfloor$.

\paragraph{Invariant.}
As long as the per--cycle step stays small, $\theta_i(t)$ is continuous and
\begin{equation}
\theta_i(t)\equiv\tilde q_i(t)\equiv\phi^m_i(t)\pmod{2\pi}.
\end{equation}
Two continuous functions congruent mod $2\pi$ differ by a fixed integer
multiple of $2\pi$, frozen at initialisation:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:k0}
\boxed{\;\theta_i(t)=\phi^m_i(t)+2\pi k_i^0,
\qquad k_i^0=\frac{\tilde q_i(0)-\phi^m_i(0)}{2\pi}\in\Z.\;}
\end{equation}
From \eqref{eq:shift}, $k_i^0=\big[\,q^{hw}_i(0)<0\,\big]$ at start
(plus any later $\pm1$ injected by a misfire, Section~\ref{sec:misfire}).

\section{The desired position and the control law}
\label{sec:law}

The desired trajectory state is copied \emph{raw} into the controller
(\texttt{hardware\_interface\_adapter\_impl.h:149}); it is \emph{not}
passed through \eqref{eq:shift} or \eqref{eq:update}. Write its frame as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:qd}
q^d_i(t)=\phi^d_i(t)+2\pi\,m_i(t),
\end{equation}
where $m_i(t)\in\Z$ is the (integer) branch chosen by the trajectory
controller's own unwrapping convention (Section~\ref{sec:jtc}).

The effort law (\texttt{src/joint\_space/controller.cpp:196}) is
\begin{equation}
\bm\tau=-K\big(\bm\theta^{\mathrm{nom}}_{\mathrm{prev}}-\bm q^d-K_s^{-1}\bm g\big)
        -D\big(\dot{\bm\theta}^{\mathrm{nom}}_{\mathrm{prev}}-\dot{\bm q}^d\big),
\end{equation}
with $K$ the joint stiffness, $D$ the damping, $K_s^{-1}$ the inverse joint
stiffness and $\bm g$ gravity. The proportional position error is
$\bm e=\bm\theta^{\mathrm{nom}}_{\mathrm{prev}}-\bm q^d$. Only the
\emph{integral} state $\bm q_{\mathrm{error,sum}}$ is clamped
(\texttt{controller.cpp:273}); $\bm e$ itself is \textbf{not} clamped.

\section{Error decomposition}

In tracking $\theta^{\mathrm{nom}}_i\approx\theta_i$, so combining
\eqref{eq:k0} and \eqref{eq:qd},
\begin{align}
e_i &= \theta_i - q^d_i
     = \big(\phi^m_i+2\pi k_i^0\big)-\big(\phi^d_i+2\pi m_i\big)\notag\\
\label{eq:err}
    &= \boxed{\;\underbrace{\big(\phi^m_i-\phi^d_i\big)}_{\text{true tracking error}}
        \;+\;2\pi\big(k_i^0-m_i(t)\big).\;}
\end{align}
The first term is what we want. The second is a spurious integer number of
full turns, present whenever the measured frame ($k_i^0$, set by the
$+2\pi$ shift) and the desired frame ($m_i$, set by the trajectory
controller) disagree.

\section{Why it spins, and only in effort mode}

The proportional torque on joint $i$ is
\begin{equation}
\tau_i \supset -K_{ii} e_i
   = -K_{ii}\big(\phi^m_i-\phi^d_i\big)
     \;\underbrace{-\,2\pi K_{ii}\big(k_i^0-m_i(t)\big)}_{\tau_i^{\mathrm{spurious}}}.
\end{equation}
Each unit of $k_i^0-m_i$ contributes a \emph{constant} torque of magnitude
$2\pi K_{ii}$ in a fixed direction. Because $\bm e$ is unclamped, this
reaches the actuator in full. As the joint accelerates, $\phi^m_i$ runs
away but $\theta_i$ tracks it continuously via \eqref{eq:update}, so $e_i$
does \emph{not} shrink --- the ``unwinding'' never completes and the joint
sustains rotation. In a position mode this law never executes, hence the
effort--mode exclusivity.

\section{The threshold misfire (the intermittency)}
\label{sec:misfire}

The wrap branch of \eqref{eq:update} triggers on $|t-c_i|\ge\Theta=\tfrac{3\pi}{2}$.
Let the effective per--cycle increment be
$\Delta_i=|\dot\phi^m_i|\,\Delta t_{\mathrm{eff}}$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Correct} unwrapping requires $\Delta_i<\tfrac{\pi}{2}$
        (a genuine $2\pi$ wrap then yields $|t-c_i|=2\pi-\Delta_i\ge\tfrac{3\pi}{2}$).
  \item A \textbf{false wrap} occurs when $\Delta_i\ge\tfrac{3\pi}{2}$ with no
        boundary crossing: a large in--band step is misread as a wrap, and
        \begin{equation}
        \theta_i\mathrel{+}=\Nrm(t)-\Nrm(c_i)=(t-c_i)\mp 2\pi,
        \end{equation}
        injecting $\mp2\pi$, i.e. flipping $k_i^0\to k_i^0\mp1$ in
        \eqref{eq:k0}--\eqref{eq:err}.
\end{itemize}
With the Gen3 limit $|\dot\phi^m_i|\le 1.3963\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, a false wrap
needs $\Delta t_{\mathrm{eff}}\ge\frac{3\pi/2}{1.3963}\approx 3.4\,\mathrm{s}$ in a
single cycle --- i.e. a dropped or severely late real--time cycle. This is
intermittent by nature and accounts for ``every now and then.''

\section{The unverified link: trajectory and driver conventions}
\label{sec:jtc}

Equation~\eqref{eq:err} shows the offset is $k_i^0-m_i(t)$. The value of
$m_i(t)$ depends on the upstream
\texttt{joint\_trajectory\_controller}: for \texttt{continuous} joints
(\texttt{angle\_wraparound = true}) it shifts each waypoint by the multiple
of $2\pi$ that makes the path continuous with the \emph{current measured
position}, then samples a continuous desired. Thus $m_i$ is a (slowly
varying / piecewise constant) integer chosen about the \emph{raw}
$q^{hw}_i$ origin --- \emph{not} about the $+2\pi$--shifted, extended
$\theta_i$ origin used by the compliant controller. The two origins differ
by exactly $k_i^0$ from \eqref{eq:shift}.

How often $k_i^0-m_i\neq 0$ depends on the kortex driver's reported range:
\begin{itemize}
  \item driver reports $[0,2\pi)$: the shift \eqref{eq:shift} never fires,
        $k_i^0=0$ initially, and the residual failures come from the
        misfire/drift path of Section~\ref{sec:misfire};
  \item driver reports $[-\pi,\pi)$: the shift fires for any joint sitting
        negative, giving a near--deterministic $2\pi$ offset right after start.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{To verify:} the installed JTC source (waypoint wraparound origin)
and the kortex hardware interface's reported joint range. These set the
\emph{frequency}, not the \emph{existence}, of the failure.

\section{Fix}

Two independent changes; either alone removes most of the risk, both
together close it.

\paragraph{(1) Frame--consistent desired.}
Before differencing, replace $q^d_i$ with the representative congruent to
$q^d_i$ and nearest to $\theta^{\mathrm{nom}}_i$:
\begin{equation}
\hat q^d_i = q^d_i + 2\pi\,\round\!\left(\frac{\theta^{\mathrm{nom}}_i-q^d_i}{2\pi}\right),
\qquad
e_i=\theta^{\mathrm{nom}}_i-\hat q^d_i\in[-\pi,\pi].
\end{equation}
This cancels the $2\pi(k_i^0-m_i)$ term in \eqref{eq:err} identically,
leaving $e_i=\Nrm(\phi^m_i-\phi^d_i)$ --- the true error taken the short way.

\paragraph{(2) Clamp the proportional error.}
As an independent safety net, bound $\bm e$ the way the integral term is
already bounded:
\begin{equation}
e_i \leftarrow \clip\big(e_i,\,-e^{\max}_i,\,e^{\max}_i\big),
\end{equation}
so even a residual misfire can never command more than $K_{ii}e^{\max}_i$.

\paragraph{(3) Robuster wrap detection (optional).}
Replace the fixed threshold $\Theta=\tfrac{3\pi}{2}$ by a velocity/period
aware test, e.g.\ flag a wrap only when $|t-c_i|>2\pi-\kappa$ with
$\kappa$ a margin larger than the worst plausible $\Delta_i$, removing the
false--wrap path of Section~\ref{sec:misfire}.

The same pattern (measured unwrapped, desired raw, unclamped $\bm e$)
exists in the \texttt{joint\_task\_space} and \texttt{task\_space}
controllers and should receive the same treatment.
